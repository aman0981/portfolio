# §14 — 30-Day LinkedIn Plan

Built against your real target set in `docs/resume/job-search/2026-10-04-job-shortlist.md`, not a generic
template. Every action below is something you can actually do, with a time estimate.

> **This plan runs alongside your existing two-week application sprint, not instead of it.** That sprint is about
> *outbound* applications. This is about *inbound* and *referrals* — the channel your own shortlist identified as
> decisive: *"One referral beats 20 cold applications."*

## Open to Work — configure this in Week 1, not later

**Setting: "Share with recruiters only."** Per your decision. Your profile joins LinkedIn Recruiter's
*Open to Work* talent pool, which many recruiters search exclusively, without the green `#OpenToWork` photo frame
that your colleagues and Engineo would see.

> **Honest caveat:** recruiters-only is not leak-proof. LinkedIn excludes recruiters whose accounts are
> associated with your current employer, but the mechanism depends on correct company linkage and does not cover
> every edge case — a colleague using a personal Recruiter seat, for instance. The risk is low and widely
> accepted, but it is not zero. If Engineo has an in-house talent team using LinkedIn Recruiter, weigh that.

### Recommended values

| Field | Value | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Job titles** (max 5) | `Software Engineer` · `Backend Developer` · `Python Developer` · `Data Engineer` · `Data Extraction Specialist` | The first three cover the highest-volume, best-paying searches in your shortlist. LinkedIn autocompletes from standardised titles, so "Web Scraping Engineer" may not be selectable — pick the closest offered scraping/extraction title. |
| **Locations** (max 5) | `Gurugram, Haryana` · `New Delhi, Delhi` · `Noida, Uttar Pradesh` · `India` (for nationwide remote) | Covers NCR onsite/hybrid plus remote-India. Do not add cities you would not move to; it pollutes your match quality. |
| **Workplace** | On-site · Hybrid · **Remote** — select all three | Your shortlist's best-paying roles are remote (Ottimate, Wynd Labs, GitLab, Supabase). Excluding remote would cut your top of market. |
| **Job types** | **Full-time** and **Contract** | Searchlook and Wynd Labs are contractor arrangements you flagged as acceptable. Leave Part-time, Internship and Temporary off. |
| **Start date** | Actively looking, **can start in 30 days** | Your notice period is an asset. Your own shortlist says to state it early. |
| **Experience level** (job alerts) | Associate **and** Mid-Senior level | You have ~2 years. Entry-level alerts will flood you with fresher roles; Director+ is noise. Mid-Senior catches the 2–5 year band where most of your Tier A sits. |
| **Industries to follow** | Software Development · IT Services & IT Consulting · Technology, Information & Internet · Travel Arrangements | The last one is not padding — four shortlisted employers are travel-domain, and you have genuine travel experience. |

## Week 2 — Authority and credibility

The profile is now accurate. This week makes it *believed*.

| Day | Action | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **8** | Ask **5 colleagues** to endorse only your three pinned skills — Python, FastAPI, Web Scraping. Send the actual skill names in the message; vague asks get ignored. | Concentrated endorsements on three skills outweigh scattered ones across fifty. |
| **9** | Request **2 recommendations**: your current manager at Engineo (ask them to mention the promotion and the AWS portal ownership) and one peer from the US travel-client project. | Recommendations are the slowest asset to acquire and the hardest to fake. Start now; they take 1–2 weeks to arrive. |
| **10** | Write **post 1**: the HireBeacon close-out gate. "My crawler nearly deleted 69,318 live job listings. Here's the 20-line guard that stopped it." Explain the <50%-of-baseline rule. | Concrete, technical, has a number, and demonstrates judgement rather than tool knowledge. |
| **11** | Tidy your **GitHub**: a proper README on HireBeacon and XBRL_Engine with a screenshot and a one-line pitch, pinned to your profile. | Every recruiter who clicks Featured lands there. A bare repo undoes the profile. |
| **12** | Comment substantively on **5 posts** from engineers at shortlisted companies (Atlys, Safe Security, GobbleCube, Anaplan). Real technical replies, not "Great post!" | Warms you up before any referral ask. People accept requests from names they recognise. |
| **13–14** | Write **post 2**: multi-browser-engine framework upgrade — why one driver per target fails and what changed. | Establishes the extraction specialism in public, where it is searchable and shareable. |

**Posting rule:** two posts a week, maximum. Technical specifics with a number. No motivational content, no
"thrilled to announce", no engagement bait. Your audience is engineers and technical recruiters, and both
discount the genre instantly.

## What success looks like at Day 30

Honest targets, based on a profile going from near-invisible to fully populated in an active market:

| Metric | Today | Realistic Day 30 |
|---|---|---|
| Search appearances / week | Low — the profile fails most filters | **3–8×** today |
| Profile views / week | Low | **3–5×** today |
| Inbound recruiter messages | Rare | 2–6 per month |
| Referral conversations | 0 | 3–6 started |

**These are directional estimates, not promises.** They depend on hiring volume, your activity level and who
happens to be searching. The profile controls your eligibility and your conversion; it does not control demand.

### The two things that matter more than everything else here

1. **Fill the current role and add the skills.** Days 1–2. If you do nothing else in the thirty days, do those.
   They are worth more than all four weeks of posting combined.
2. **Referrals.** Your own shortlist already concluded this. The profile exists to make the referral ask land —
   when someone forwards your name internally, the first thing their colleague does is open your LinkedIn.
   Right now that would work against you.
